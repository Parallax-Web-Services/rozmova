Three standards

The same word, spelled three ways

Romanization is not one thing. The national system is what a passport and a road sign use, and it drops information on purpose. The learner variant keeps what you need in order to pronounce the word.

UkrainianNationalLearnerBGN/PCGN

Why the learner column differs. The national system drops the soft sign, so Львів becomes Lviv and nothing tells you the l is palatalized — a reader says it hard, where Ukrainian says it soft. The learner column keeps that mark: Lʹviv. Dropping it is right for a passport, where one settled spelling per name matters more than pronunciation, and wrong for someone learning to say the word.

The learner column is a proposal, not a standard, and the table above shows what it costs: dropping the positional rules so that each letter has one stable value turns Київ into Kyyiv and Юрій into Yurij. Predictable to decode, unfamiliar to read. Whether that trade is worth making is a decision about who the reader is.

Editorial layer

Where the standard and usage disagree

KMU 2010 romanizes Зеленський as Zelenskyi. The Office of the President writes Zelenskyy, and that is the spelling in circulation. Both are defensible, so a publication picks one and applies it everywhere — including mid-sentence, where the source reads Зеленського or Зеленським. Ukrainian declines; English does not.

Why
Every override records its reasoning. Nothing here is a hidden find-and-replace. Each entry carries a note saying who decided and on what grounds, and the engine reports which rules fired on any given pass — so a reader who questions a spelling can be shown the rule instead of asked to trust it. The same layer applies to Russian-language sources, where Киев publishes as Kyiv, while Белгород stays Belgorod. Whose place it is decides the form, not which language the sentence was written in.

Rozmova is an open-source core: three Ukrainian schemes, three Russian ones, an editorial preference layer, and an archival pipeline. The national scheme is verified against every example in the official Cabinet of Ministers table, and the JavaScript running this page is held byte-identical to the PHP library across 716 comparisons.
